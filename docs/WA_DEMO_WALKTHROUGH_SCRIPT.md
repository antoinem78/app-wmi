# The ninety second WhatsApp walkthrough: shot list and script

**Purpose:** one recording, attachable to a pitch, that shows an enquiry arriving with its source attached. Nobody believes attribution described in prose. They believe it when they watch a click become a named lead.

**Rules it obeys:** spoken as Anthony, first person singular. No mention of any tool, platform, endpoint or agent by name. No banned claim from the register. The volume caveat is spoken, not skipped, because a demo that hides its limits gets found out on the first busy day.

**Setup before you press record.** Two windows side by side: the website on the left, the CRM enquiry list on the right, filtered to today and empty. Phone screen mirrored or filmed separately for the WhatsApp step.

**The click id in the address bar.** Use a real one, not a test string, because a viewer who pauses the video will read it. We have no paid traffic to our own site, so the practical source is a genuine ad click made in the course of work you were doing anyway; the click is already paid for, so reusing the identifier costs nobody anything further.

**Strip it before it goes on camera.** A landing URL from someone else's ad arrives carrying their campaign name, their campaign id and a pile of tracking parameters. Keep the `gclid` and delete everything else. The gclid is opaque and identifies nobody to a viewer; `utm_campaign` and `gad_campaignid` are another company's confidential data and have no business in a document you send to prospects. One identifier in the address bar also reads better than four.

So the demo URL is `https://www.wmiltd.com/?gclid=<the real value>` and nothing more.

**Delete the demo lead afterwards.** A gclid belongs to the account whose ad was clicked. Harmless today, because nothing uploads offline conversions from this site. Once something does, a demo lead marked won would push a conversion against a click id we do not own. Google would reject it and nothing would break, but deleting the record means the question never comes up.

## Shot 1, 0:00 to 0:12. The problem, on screen

**Show:** the website, ordinary, nothing special.

> "Someone clicks your advert, lands on your site, and taps WhatsApp. The moment they leave for the messaging app, everything you knew about them is gone. You get a message from a phone number. You have no idea which advert paid for it."

## Shot 2, 0:12 to 0:25. The click, with its source visible

**Show:** the address bar, with the campaign tracking on the end of the URL. Zoom it. Then tap the WhatsApp button.

> "So watch what happens instead. I have arrived here from a specific advert. You can see it in the address. I tap the button."

## Shot 3, 0:25 to 0:40. The message goes, deliberately stripped

**Show:** WhatsApp opening with the prefilled message. **Delete the prefilled text.** Type something ordinary in its place. Send.

> "Now here is the part everyone gets wrong. Most systems put a reference code in this message and hope nobody deletes it. People delete it. So I am going to delete it, and type my own message, exactly as a real customer would."

This shot is the whole demo. Do not skip the deletion. It is the difference between a claim and a proof.

## Shot 4, 0:40 to 0:58. The lead appears

**Show:** the CRM list, empty, then the new enquiry appearing. Let the silence run for the two or three seconds it actually takes. Do not cut.

> "And there it is. A new enquiry, with the advert it came from attached, even though nothing survived inside the message."

## Shot 5, 0:58 to 1:15. What that unlocks

**Show:** the lead record, then the pipeline with a won deal carrying a value.

> "When that enquiry turns into a booking and you mark it won, the advertising platforms are told which advert produced it and what it was worth. That is the part that compounds. They stop chasing clicks and start finding people who buy."

## Shot 6, 1:15 to 1:30. The honest bit

**Show:** the attribution label on the lead record, including one marked as unknown if you have one.

> "One thing I will always be straight about. Every enquiry is labelled with how it was matched, so you can see your own coverage rather than taking my word for it. When several people enquire in the same short window, it does not guess. It marks those unknown. I would rather tell you I do not know than credit the wrong advert, because you are going to spend money on these numbers."

**End card:** name, and one line. No logo animation, no music swell.

## Two things that will ruin the take

**Do not use a synthetic click id.** The only click record in the system today is a test one from 6 August with a made up value. A prospect who pauses on that frame sees a demo, not a system. Equally, do not invent a realistic-looking one: that is worse than an obviously fake one, because it dresses a mock up as real and the damage if anyone checks is out of all proportion to what it bought you.

**Do not edit out the wait.** The two or three seconds between sending and the lead appearing is the most persuasive footage in the video. Cutting it makes it look staged.

## After the recording

The same setup doubles as a live demo, where the prospect uses their own phone and watches their own enquiry appear. That converts better than any video and costs one afternoon to make repeatable. The recording is what you attach; the live version is what you do on a call.
