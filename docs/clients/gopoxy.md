**Meta is not a problem.** Across five fortnights from 1 June its ROAS runs 3.49, 2.41, 3.72, 4.91, 3.93. The latest is its second best. Google's 3.34 is its worst against a prior range of 4.10 to 5.86, so Google is the outlier and Meta is normal.

### Why the 2026-08-15 reading was wrong, and the method lesson

The first pass compared 16-30 July against 31 July-14 August and reported a step-down on both platforms caused by basket value. **16-30 July was an exceptional fortnight, not a baseline**: store AOV £105 and 4.02 units per order, against ~£84 and ~3.1 in both the fortnight before and the fortnight after. Measuring from a peak manufactured a collapse on Meta that never happened and overstated Google's.

Three compounding errors, all mine, all in window choice or arithmetic:
1. A first Meta cross-check compared 8-14 August against 1-7 August, both **after** the suspected break, and wrongly cleared Meta as stable.
2. The corrected A/B pass then used a **peak** as its baseline, inverting the conclusion the other way.
3. A product-level table overwrote rather than accumulated rows per product id, briefly showing the best SKU going dark when it had not.

**The rule that would have caught all three: before trusting any two-window comparison, plot at least four consecutive windows and confirm the baseline is typical.** Two points cannot distinguish a fall from a return to normal.

### The AOV question, closed

There is no unexplained basket-value collapse. The store's units per order is ~3.1 in early July and ~3.1 in August; the 4.02 in 16-30 July is the anomaly to explain, not the August figure. Nothing here needs a question to Ryan about a site or offer change on 31 July, and the earlier out-of-stock hypothesis is not needed to explain anything. **Do not send that question.**
