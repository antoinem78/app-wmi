Nurture copy written in Anthony's first person is right, and it creates a specific temptation: lines like "I had a look at your website", "I was just thinking about your account", "I have pulled your numbers". Those are the highest-converting sentences in the draft and they must not go in a workflow, because no one looked.

**Why:** the bluff gets called on the first reply. The moment the contact asks a follow-up question about the thing you claimed to have looked at, the sender either has to scramble or admit the message was automated, and both are worse than never having claimed it. It also puts a false statement in Anthony's name, which is the same failure mode as inventing a tax date in Kyle's name in the VIP sequence, and a bigger one than a wrong figure because it is about attention rather than data.

**How to apply:** automated messages can be warm, specific, first person, and can name the offer precisely ("fifteen minutes, no charge, I will tell you where the budget is going"). They cannot assert that a human has already spent time on this contact. Sweep every drafted sequence for past-tense first-person verbs about the recipient's business and check each one against what the workflow actually does before it sends. If a line genuinely requires prior work, either make the workflow trigger only after that work is logged, or move the line to a manual send.
